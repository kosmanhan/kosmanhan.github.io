Das Kricket

Written on: November 30, 2025

There is talk of changes to test championships: https://www.espncricinfo.com/story/icc-news-two-tier-wtc-model-scuppered-odi-super-league-revival-on-the-cards-1510711

Currently, there are two-year cycles, in which 9 teams contest. There is no order to which teams have home series and which have away. The top two teams play a final test for the championship. I don't like this format. I also don't like the changes proposed.

The t20 championship occurs every even year. In 2028, the Olympics will also have cricket, in the t20 format. The ODI World Cup will be in 2027 and every four years after. It makes sense to have the t20 championship in the non-Olympics even years.

I would want a four year, round-robin test cycle to start from 2029. That way, every year would have an event: t20 championship, ODI World Cup, Olympics, New Test Cycle. There is precedent to a four-year test cycle, as the Ashes occur in England and Australia every four years. The main difference with the Ashes would be that a series win would be determined by matches both home and away, with away wins for a team breaking a tie. In total, each team would have twelve series. There would be no final. The team with the most series wins would win. The lowest ranked team would be relegated.

Promotion to the cycle would come from an Intercontinental ODI Cup, which would be contested from five teams from five regions of the world: Amerikas, Afrika, Europe, Asia-Pacifik, Indian Subcontinent. It will be round-robin and the team with the most wins will be promoted to the test cycle for four years. Teams for the Intercontinental ODI Cup would come from regional ODI tournaments. The team with the most wins in a regional ODI tournament wins the tournament, while the team with the most wins that is not one of the twelve teams in the test cycle, qualifies for the Intercontinental ODI Cup. There could also be an ODI Champions Trophy, with the five winners of the ODI regional tournaments, in case there is appetite for it.

This would mean ODI cricket in odd years, and t20 cricket in even years, for a maximum of 2 months per year. If that is the only international limited overs cricket, it leaves a lot of space for players to play franchise/domestic limited overs cricket.
